How he works Open the desk

The week you know

A garage full of stuff is not a store.

You take the pictures. Then the listing never happens. Or it happens once, at the wrong price, and sits. Or it sells and Facebook wants a speech about whether it sold. None of that is a selling problem. It is a stall problem.

The pile grows.

Photos live in the camera roll. Nothing is researched. Nothing is priced. Nothing is up.

The ad goes stale.

A listing that does not move needs a lower number. Walking into Facebook to type it, every week, is how it never happens.

Taking it down is a form.

Mark as sold tells the platform a story it does not need. The listing should just be gone.

Buyers want a chatbot.

They do not get one from him. A silent auto-replier on a real account is how the stall gets flagged.

Receive → retire

Five moves. One tap in the middle.

He never lists anything you did not approve. He never answers a buyer. The work is automated. The decision is not.

The desk

The phone is the floor.

Live, pipeline, sold, stuck. Open a card. Edit the title, the price, the copy. Save, confirm, he changes the live ad. Remove, confirm, the listing is deleted — never marked sold. Hide is only this phone. Mark sold is a ledger note, not a Facebook speech.

The markdown

The price moves when you say so.

Once a week he proposes a 10% drop, capped at the floor you set. You get a card. Approve it and he updates the desk and the live Facebook listing. Reject it and the number stays. He will not chase a listing under the floor, and he will not nag the same item twice in seven days.

Take-down

Gone. Not sold.

Remove deletes the listing. Facebook is not told whether it sold. People who already messaged you see that it is no longer listed. No reason. No form. If the platform asks why, the only answer he will pick is that you no longer want to sell it.

What he will not do

The stall has rules.

No inbox.

He posts, edits, and deletes. He does not open buyer chat. He does not draft a reply unless you build that lane on purpose later.

No second account.

Real stall only. Five new listings a day. Minutes between posts. One thing in flight. A checkpoint stops the lane.

No eBay — yet.

The second venue button stays dark until that lane is actually connected. He will not pretend a post went up.

No silent yes.

Offers below the floor get a counter, never an accept, and only if you are the one sending the message.

This is a private operator, not a kit on the shelf. The desk lives on the Mothership. The art on this page is his. When the stall changes, this page changes with it.
